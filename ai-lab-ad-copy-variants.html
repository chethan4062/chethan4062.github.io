Ad Copy Variants

A Claude skill that turns a product page or brief into ready-to-use ad copy for Meta, Google Search, Performance Max, Demand Gen and short video, with every line checked against character limits.

Built September 2026 · Works from a product page link or a short brief

See sample output Download the skill
01 — The problem

Five platforms, five sets of limits, one idea.

Writing ad copy for every platform means juggling different character limits, rewriting the same idea five ways, and checking claims by hand. Most variants end up as rewordings of one idea, which gives Meta's and Google's systems nothing real to test.

02 — What I built

A full copy set from one product page.

A Claude skill that builds a brief from a product page, confirms it with you, then writes a full copy set built on 5 distinct angles.

  • Meta: 5 primary texts, 5 headlines, 5 descriptions
  • Google Search: 15 headlines, 4 descriptions and 2 URL paths for a responsive search ad
  • Performance Max and Demand Gen: full text asset sets
  • Video: 5 hooks for the first 3 seconds and 2–3 short Reels/Shorts scripts
  • Character counts on every line, checked by a script
  • A fact check against the brief, a claims-to-verify list, and a first test plan
03 — Tools used

What it is built on.

Claude Claude Skills Python (length checker) Meta Ads Google Ads
04 — How it works

Four steps, product page to copy set.

  1. 1
    Share a product page
    Or a short brief: product, audience, offer, proof, tone.
  2. 2
    Confirm the brief
    Claude pulls the facts from the page and checks them with you first.
  3. 3
    Write on 5 angles
    For example: how it works, proof, objection handling, offer and lifestyle.
  4. 4
    Check and test
    Lengths, facts and claims are checked, then it suggests what to test first.
Built-in rules

Different angles, not rewordings · Every fact traced to the brief · No invented reviews, ratings or discounts · Risky claims flagged for review.

Video hooks and scripts

Hooks for the first 3 seconds (5)
  1. [How it works] Spoken: "Four UV filters. One bottle." · On-screen: "4 UV filters, 1 layer"
  2. [Proof] Spoken: "It says SPF 50. The lab measured 56.6." · On-screen: "Lab-tested: SPF 56.6"
  3. [Objection] Spoken: "Watch this rub in. No white cast." (swatch demo) · On-screen: "Zero white cast?"
  4. [Offer] Spoken: "Buy two, the third one's free." · On-screen: "Buy 2, Get 3rd Free"
  5. [Lifestyle] Spoken: "What if your moisturiser was your sunscreen?" · On-screen: "Moisturiser meets SPF 50"
Script 1 — Proof, ~25 seconds
  • 0–3s Hook: Bottle to camera. "It says SPF 50. The lab measured 56.6."
  • 3–10s Proof: "Tested in-vivo by an independent lab, to international standard ISO 24444." Show the lab-report numbers from the product page.
  • 10–20s Product and benefit: "Four UV filters for UVA and UVB, PA++++, and no fragrance." Swatch on the back of the hand, blended in.
  • 20–25s Offer and CTA: "Buy 2, get the 3rd free. Code B2G3RDFREE."

On-screen text: "Lab-tested: SPF 56.6" → "4 UV filters · PA++++" → "Buy 2, Get 3rd Free"

Script 2 — Objection handling (demo), ~20 seconds
  • 0–3s Hook: A dollop on the cheek. "Watch this rub in."
  • 3–10s Demo: Blend in real time and show no white cast. "No white cast, no sticky feel."
  • 10–17s Product and benefit: "Minimalist SPF 50. Fragrance-free, non-comedogenic, spreads like a light moisturiser."
  • 17–20s CTA: "Buy 2, get the 3rd free."

On-screen text: "Zero white cast?" → "Fragrance-free · Non-comedogenic" → "Buy 2, Get 3rd Free"

Script 3 — Lifestyle, ~20 seconds
  • 0–3s Hook: A morning shelf. "What if your moisturiser was your sunscreen?"
  • 3–10s Problem: "Every morning is serum, moisturiser, sunscreen, and three layers of feeling heavy."
  • 10–17s Product: "Minimalist SPF 50 feels like a light moisturiser, with Vitamins B3, B5, E and F."
  • 17–20s CTA: "One for home, one for your bag, the third one's free."

On-screen text: "Moisturiser meets SPF 50" → "Vitamins B3, B5, E, F" → "Buy 2, Get 3rd Free"

Claims to verify

  • "Buy 2, Get the 3rd Free": confirm which products it covers, which item is free, and the end date. Pause offer lines when the code expires (ASCI expects price accuracy).
  • "Lab-tested at SPF 56.6": supported by the product page (ISO 24444:2019). Keep the report handy in case Meta or ASCI asks for it.
  • "No white cast": claimed on the page, but some reviews mention a slight cast on some skin tones. Test the demo creative across skin tones before scaling.
  • "Moisturiser-like": a texture claim from the page. Avoid implying it replaces a moisturiser.
  • The product isn't recommended during pregnancy or lactation, so exclude those audiences.
  • "Repairs skin" from the page was left out on purpose because it reads as a treatment claim.

What to test first

  • Proof vs objection (Meta primary text 2 vs 3): shows whether this audience buys on lab credibility or on sensory reassurance.
  • Hook test (Script 1 body with Hook 2 vs Hook 3): shows whether a number or a live demo stops the scroll better. Judge on 3-second view rate.
  • Offer vs no offer (Meta headline 4 vs 2, same creative): shows whether Buy 2 Get 1 lifts conversion rate enough to justify the lower margin, or whether proof alone converts.
06 — What I learned

What building this taught me.

  • Character limits are the easy part; the hard part is writing angles that are genuinely different.
  • Pulling the brief from the product page gives better proof points than a typed brief, but it needs a confirmation step.
  • In the first test the skill added a detail that wasn't on the page, so I added a fact-check step: every detail must trace back to the brief.
  • Flagging claims before they run matters most in health, beauty and finance.
