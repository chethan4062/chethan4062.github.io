Weekly Ads Summary

A Google Sheet template and a Claude skill that turn a week of Meta and Google Ads data into a detailed, action-focused team update in minutes.

Built September 2026 · Reads the sheet through Google Drive or an uploaded file

See sample summary Download the template Download the skill
01 — The problem

The Monday update eats the morning.

Every Monday, someone pulls last week's numbers, compares them with the week before, tries to remember what changed, and writes an update. It takes an hour or more, and the "why" behind the numbers often gets lost.

02 — What I built

A sheet and a skill that work together.

A simple report sheet and a Claude skill that writes the weekly update from it.

The template

Daily Data (one row per day per campaign, with CTR, CPC, CPA and ROAS calculated) · Targets (CPA or ROAS goal and weekly budget per platform) · Change Log (budget, bid and creative changes) · Weekly View (week-on-week totals).

The skill

Compares the latest complete week with the week before, explains movements using the Change Log and the metrics, flags issues, and writes prioritised action items plus a short Slack version.

03 — Tools used

What it is built on.

Claude Claude Skills Google Sheets Google Drive connector Python (pandas) Meta Ads Google Ads
04 — How it works

Four steps, sheet to team update.

  1. 1
    Fill the sheet
    Paste daily data, set targets, and log any changes you make.
  2. 2
    Ask Claude
    Write this week's ads summary from my Weekly Ads Report sheet.
  3. 3
    Analyse
    A script calculates week-on-week changes, target gaps, pacing and zero-conversion days.
  4. 4
    Get the update
    Scoreboard, what changed and why, movers, issues, action items and a Slack version.
How it explains changes

Checks tracking first, then conversion rate, CTR, CPC and spend changes, then the Change Log · Names a cause only when the data supports it · Every issue leads to an action.

What changed and why

  • Meta tracking gap on 17 Sep. Both Meta campaigns recorded 0 conversions that day, despite 1,035 clicks. Last week they averaged about 11 per day. That day matches the "Website checkout update deployed" entry in the Change Log, and conversions came back on 18 Sep. Excluding 17 Sep, Meta CPA is ₹467, which is still above target.
  • Meta Prospecting fatigue. CPA roughly doubled from ₹417 to ₹811, and ROAS fell from 2.69x to 1.42x. CTR dropped 22% (1.09% to 0.85%) while CPC rose 37%, starting on 14 Sep, before the checkout release. The Change Log shows no new creatives were launched this week. Even excluding 17 Sep, CPA is ₹704.
  • Google PMax scaled well. The Change Log shows a 15% budget increase. Spend rose 14%, conversions rose 26% (61 to 77) and ROAS improved from 3.92x to 4.22x.
  • Meta Retargeting dip is mostly the tracking gap. Its CPA rose from ₹280 to ₹331. Excluding 17 Sep it is ₹286, and CTR held steady at 1.85%.
  • Google Generic is still below target after the bidding switch. It moved to Target ROAS 3.5x on 7 Sep. ROAS improved slightly (2.83x to 2.96x) but is still under 3.5x, at only 22 conversions per week.

06 — What I learned

What building this taught me.

  • A change log is what turns a report into an explanation: most week-on-week movements trace back to something the team did.
  • Testing on sample data with planted problems (fatigue and a tracking gap) is a quick way to check the skill catches what matters.
  • In the first test, the skill treated a normal low day as a possible tracking issue, so I added a rule: only flag anomalies that meet a clear threshold.
  • Letting a script do the maths and Claude do the explaining keeps the numbers reliable.
